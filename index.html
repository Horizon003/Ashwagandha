Botanical & Pharmacognostic Profile

Botanical Name
Withania somnifera (L.) Dunal
Family
Solanaceae
Common Names
Ashwagandha, Indian Ginseng, Winter Cherry
Part Used
Dried roots & stem bases
Taste
Bitter, Astringent (Tikta, Kashaya)
Category
Rasayana — Rejuvenative adaptogen
Habitat & Distribution
Cultivated in dry regions of Pakistan, India, Middle East & Africa. Prefers arid, sandy soils with minimal rainfall.
HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
~3000 BCE
Charaka Samhita — First Mention
Documented as a Rasayana in the Charaka Samhita, one of the oldest Ayurvedic texts, prescribing it for longevity, memory enhancement, and vitality.
~600 BCE
Sushruta Samhita — Surgical Classic
Included in Sushruta Samhita for wound healing and tissue regeneration. Classified as a Balya (strength-promoting) and Vajikara (aphrodisiac) drug.
10th–14th Century CE
Islamic Golden Age — Arabian Medicine
Arab physicians including Ibn Sina referenced Ashwagandha as a strengthening herb. Integrated into Unani medicine as Asgandh for nervous debility and pain.
2000s CE — Modern Era
Pharmacological Validation
Modern research isolates withanolides as key bioactive compounds. Clinical trials confirm anti-stress, immunomodulatory, and neuroprotective effects, validating 3,000 years of traditional use.

TRADITIONAL & COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
Works Gradually
✓ TRUE
Tap to learn
✓ FACT
Ashwagandha shows noticeable effects after 4–8 weeks of consistent daily use. It is an adaptogen — not an instant fix. Patience and consistency are key.
Only for Gym-goers?
✗ MYTH
Tap to learn
✗ FALSE
Ashwagandha benefits everyone — men, women, elderly. It supports hormonal balance, thyroid function, stress relief, and cognitive health, not just athletic performance.
Completely Safe?
✗ MYTH
Tap to learn
✗ CAUTION
High doses may cause GI upset, sedation, or diarrhea. It may interact with thyroid, immunosuppressant, and sedative medications. Avoid in pregnancy.
More Dose = Better?
✗ WRONG
Tap to learn
✗ INCORRECT
Recommended dose is 300–600 mg/day of standardised extract. Exceeding this increases adverse effects without added benefits. Always follow clinical guidance.

CONCLUSION

Withania somnifera is an important medicinal plant of the Solanaceae family. Its rich withanolide content supports both traditional Ayurvedic uses and modern pharmacological applications, especially in stress management, immune support, and neuroprotection.
