Botanical & Pharmacognostic Profile

Botanical Name
Withania somnifera (L.) Dunal
Family
Solanaceae
Common Names
Ashwagandha, Indian Ginseng, Winter Cherry
Part Used
Dried roots & stem bases
Taste
Bitter, Astringent (Tikta, Kashaya)
Category
Rasayana — Rejuvenative adaptogen
Habitat & Distribution
Cultivated in dry regions of Pakistan, India, Middle East & Africa. Prefers arid, sandy soils with minimal rainfall.
HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
~3000 BCE
Charaka Samhita — First Mention
Documented as a Rasayana in the Charaka Samhita, prescribing it for longevity, memory enhancement, and vitality.
~600 BCE
Sushruta Samhita — Surgical Classic
Included in Sushruta Samhita for wound healing. Classified as Balya (strength-promoting) and Vajikara.
10th–14th C. CE
Islamic Golden Age
Ibn Sina referenced Ashwagandha as a strengthening herb. Integrated into Unani medicine as Asgandh.
2000s — Modern
Pharmacological Validation
Modern research isolates withanolides. Trials confirm anti-stress, immunomodulatory, and neuroprotective effects.

Chemical Constituents

Withanolides
Withaferin A Withanolide D Withanone
Primary active class · Adaptogenic & anti-tumor
Alkaloids
Somniferine Anaferine Withanine
Sedative & CNS relaxant effects
Glycosides
Withanosides I–VII Sitoindosides
Neuroprotective & immunomodulatory
Flavonoids & Sterols
β-Sitosterol Kaempferol Quercetin
Antioxidant & cardiovascular support
Withanolides are responsible for most pharmacological activities. Standard extracts contain ~5% withanolides.

CONCLUSION

Withania somnifera is an important medicinal plant of the Solanaceae family. Its rich withanolide content supports both traditional Ayurvedic uses and modern pharmacological applications, especially in stress management, immune support, and neuroprotection.
